# References by Feature

Only sources that directly shaped a built feature are listed. Feature numbers match `FEATURES.md`.

## 1. Automatic dose confirmation
- Fanaroff et al., 2026, *American Heart Journal*. [A pilot randomized clinical trial of gamification to increase medication adherence](https://consensus.app/papers/details/5d6847963e1a5aa1b3e0cfe7718e01cf/?utm_source=claude_desktop). The gamification had no effect (79.6% vs 78.6%). The authors blame the lack of "automated, inexpensive, unobtrusive measurement of daily medication-taking behavior," which is the gap Dropwise's sensor confirmation fills.
- Dropwise hardware brief (internal): beam-break timing of 2 to 15 ms, plus camera and thermopile fusion for landing.

## 3. Retry with full credit and 4. Aim coaching
- [SEE Program Randomized Clinical Trial (MAGIC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12947086/). Education that included teaching eye drop instillation technique, followed by reminders, improved glaucoma adherence.
- Gillison et al., 2018, *Health Psychology Review*. [A meta-analysis of techniques to promote motivation for health behaviour change from a self-determination theory perspective](https://consensus.app/papers/details/3ca9237aa67a566da1fb2bf9dfbc9c3f/?utm_source=claude_desktop). Building competence worked strongly in adults (g = 0.95) and barely in children (g = 0.11), which is why coaching targets adults and kid mode uses celebrations instead.
- Pettersson et al., 2020, *JMIR*. [Older Adults' Experiences of Behavior Change Support in a Digital Fall Prevention Exercise Program](https://consensus.app/papers/details/2238f1f066ce5f71b61dc3b98b2a7846/?utm_source=claude_desktop). Clear instructions were key to seniors feeling competent.

## 5. Routine anchor at setup
- Keller et al., 2021, *British Journal of Health Psychology*. [Habit formation following routine-based versus time-based cue planning](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b9aaa8be51fc5b939fddb1429fbbdc66/?utm_source=claude_desktop). Repeating a behavior on the same cue drives habit formation.
- [Multifaceted Prospective Memory Intervention to Improve Medication Adherence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4806399/). Tying medication to a salient event like breakfast, in the same place, raised adherence from 57% to 78%.
- Crum et al., 2024, *ACR Open Rheumatology*. [Leveraging Habits to Improve Adherence to Gout Medications](https://consensus.app/papers/details/c0cf2881d59c5542afc7c42ce9b3711a/?utm_source=claude_desktop). Predictable routines and context cues supported consistent adherence.
- Eaton et al., 2023, *Pediatric Nephrology*. [Medication adherence in youths with CKD: habits for success](https://consensus.app/papers/details/c33be25d38ac5808a0cafec7442e78af/?utm_source=claude_desktop). High adherers described location cues and taking medicine as "automatic."
- van de Vijver et al., 2023, *Frontiers in Psychology*. [Age differences in routine formation](https://consensus.app/papers/details/3d761ec4303f568c90bdac4494d1f6e8/?utm_source=claude_desktop). Older adults outperformed younger adults on a daily routine because the routine became automatic.
- Badawy et al., 2020, *JMIR*. [Habit Strength, Medication Adherence, and Habit-Based mHealth Interventions](https://consensus.app/papers/details/4fb5b92f9deb545b93facf69366edf84/?utm_source=claude_desktop). Habit strength was linked to adherence in 10 of 11 studies.

## 6. Cap glow reminder
- Okeke et al., 2009, *Ophthalmology*. [Interventions improve poor adherence with once daily glaucoma medications in electronically monitored patients](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19815286/). Audible and visible reminders on the dosing device, combined with education and phone reminders, raised adherence from 54% to 73%.

## 7. Cap "done" light and "already done" notice
- [Multifaceted Prospective Memory Intervention](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4806399/). A pill organizer let patients see whether they had taken their dose, which prevented accidental double doses.

## 8. Escalating reminders (skipped when the dose is done)
- [Clinical reminder alert fatigue in healthcare](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10983371/). Each extra reminder lowered the chance of acting on reminders by 30%. That finding comes from clinicians, not patients, but it's the reason reminders fire only when a dose is actually undone.

## 9. Habit phases
- Keller et al., 2021 (linked above). A median of **59 days** to peak automaticity, which is where the building phase length comes from.
- [Multifaceted Prospective Memory Intervention](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4806399/). Most gains were lost within 5 months after support ended, which is why support comes back when adherence slips instead of ending permanently.
- Fontanet et al., 2021, *BMJ Open*. [Randomised controlled trial targeting habit formation to improve medication adherence in gout](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6c44b1e28a1959c2ad5a09fde7c8d34a/?utm_source=claude_desktop). Uses the cue-reward-repetition model, where rewards matter less as the cue takes over, and an adaptive arm that adds reminders only when adherence falls below 75%. The encouragement fading after the building phase and the automatic "supporting" phase follow this design.

## 11. Caregiver view (weekly summary, alerts with an action)
- [Remote activity monitoring for family caregivers of persons living with dementia](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8684277/). Caregivers found monitoring pointless without a clear way to respond, which is why every alert includes what to do.
- [Experiences of Older Adults and Caregivers With Home Telemonitoring for Heart Failure](https://doi.org/10.2196/79797). Monitoring gave caregivers peace of mind but caused frustration when it disrupted routines, which is why the default is quiet with a weekly summary.
- [Acceptability of a Health Care App With 3 User Interfaces for Older Adults and Their Caregivers](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10034616/). Caregivers wanted fast, readable overviews.
- [STEP 4Life randomized clinical trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11350015/). A support partner got weekly performance reports, and older adults (average age 70) walked 1,699 more steps a day.

## 12. Patient-controlled sharing
- [Why Older Adults and Their Children Disagree About In-Home Surveillance Technology](https://academic.oup.com/gerontologist/article/60/5/926/5491612), *The Gerontologist*, 2020. Adult children consistently wanted more monitoring than their mothers did, and they underestimated what their mothers understood.
- [Experiences of Home-Dwelling Older Adults and Their Family Caregivers With Digital Health Services](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13255930/). Seniors prioritized autonomy and psychological comfort.
- Kang et al., 2025, *JMIR Aging*. [Exploring Smart Health Wearable Adoption Among Singaporean Older Adults](https://consensus.app/papers/details/91311d26f41d5103b38e0ad9a11d0d05/?utm_source=claude_desktop). Perceived privacy risk lowered competence, autonomy, and relatedness.

## 13. One-tap cheer
- [STEP 4Life](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11350015/) (linked above). A support partner's encouragement was part of the effective intervention.
- [Dialogue Agents that Share Family Information to Strengthen Grandparent-Grandchild Relationships](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.12310), arXiv 2026. Older adults engaged more actively and consistently when updates involved their grandchild.
- Palombi et al., 2025, *Age and Ageing*. [Motivation, psychological needs and physical activity in older adults](https://consensus.app/papers/details/0a28fa66e5335f01b7894304c0e656a2/?utm_source=claude_desktop). Relationships were central to older adults' self-driven motivation.
- Davis et al., 2021, *Journal of Clinical Nursing*. [Gamified applications for secondary prevention in patients with high CVD risk](https://consensus.app/papers/details/78e7059f6e2a5b1c8563883a15bf51c0/?utm_source=claude_desktop). Surprise and novelty were among the most enjoyed elements, which is why the cheer appears unannounced after the next drop.

## 14. Kid mode
- ['The mean mummy way': parents instilling eye drops to their young children](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7653738/). Parents described drops as a fight, and rewards and role-play helped.
- [Effectiveness of a Gamified Mobile App in Enhancing Treatment Adherence for Children With Amblyopia](https://games.jmir.org/2025/1/e60309), *JMIR Serious Games*, 2025. Rewards and immediate feedback came up in 65% of the needs-assessment responses.
- Gillison et al., 2018 (linked above). Competence-building barely moved children, so kid mode relies on celebration instead of coaching.

## 15. Doctor report
- Fanaroff et al., 2026 (linked above). The gamification arm sent reports to physicians for accountability, but those reports relied on self-reported doses. Dropwise's report uses sensor-confirmed data.
